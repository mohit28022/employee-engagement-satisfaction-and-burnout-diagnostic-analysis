# Executive Summary
### Employee Engagement, Satisfaction & Burnout Diagnostic — Palo Alto Networks

**Prepared for:** HR Leadership & Government Stakeholders
**Prepared by:** Unified Mentor HR Analytics Project

---

**The problem:** HR interventions today happen after an employee has already decided to leave. There is no
standing signal that flags disengagement or burnout while it's still actionable.

**What we built:** A composite **Engagement Index** and a weighted **Burnout Risk Score**, computed directly from
existing HR fields (satisfaction ratings, overtime, work-life balance, travel, tenure, promotion history) — no new
data collection required. Delivered as a live, filterable Streamlit dashboard for HR and managers.

**Headline finding:** Employees flagged **High burnout risk attrite at 28.7%**, versus **8.2%** for Low-risk
employees — a **3.5x difference** that is visible *before* resignation, not after.

**Scale of the opportunity:** 56% of the current workforce sits in at least Medium burnout risk. This is a much
larger, more addressable population than the ~16% who actually leave — meaning early intervention has real room to
move the needle.

**Top 3 recommendations:**
1. Make the Burnout Risk Score a standing quarterly HR metric, routed to managers for proactive check-ins.
2. Prioritize Research & Development — PANW's largest department — for a work-life balance intervention.
3. Review promotion cadence for employees 4+ years without advancement; this compounds with burnout risk.

**Deliverables:**
- Interactive Streamlit dashboard (Engagement Overview, Burnout Risk, Career Stage Analysis, Manager Action Panel)
- Full research paper with EDA, methodology, and findings
- Enriched dataset with computed KPIs, ready for ongoing tracking

**Bottom line:** This shifts HR analytics from reactive attrition reporting to preventive employee experience
diagnostics — the same underlying data, used earlier, where it can still change the outcome.
